Notes & caveats

Scope & method

This analysis uses the same corpus as the observing-mode contention map: the AASKAII book, 7 thematic sections, and roughly 189 papers. We reopened each of the 7 section research passes from that first analysis and asked two new questions of the same papers. First, what do the authors explicitly flag as unresolved? Second, what hard numbers do they quote as necessary for their science? A separate pass then ran 16 fixed technique keywords against the whole corpus, independent of section boundaries, to rank which methods are load-bearing across the most sections rather than specific to just one.

Sourcing follows the same rule throughout. We report a figure or claim only where a paper states it explicitly, and we source it back to that paper. A gap in a table cell means the paper did not state a number for that axis. It does not mean the requirement does not exist.

Common open questions

~90 sourced statements → 10 recurring clusters

This ranking counts how many of the 7 AASKAII sections independently raise the same unresolved problem. A high count means multiple independent teams flagged the same risk, much like a shared red risk on a roadmap.

3of 7 sections

Foreground contamination is the limiting systematic for 21-cm & HI intensity-mapping cosmology

AEG

Galactic synchrotron and other foregrounds sit 4–5 orders of magnitude above the cosmological 21-cm signal. Every group working on EoR power-spectrum, HI intensity mapping, or 21-cm tomography names foreground removal as the field's central unsolved problem. The difficulty is removing the foregrounds without biasing the signal itself.

  • A · Trott et al. — separating cosmology from "gastrophysics" in the 21-cm signal is named the key challenge; whether it can be "cleaned" of astrophysics at all is unclear.
  • E · Cunnington/MeerKLASS, Wolz, Spinelli — isolating the signal "without distorting or biasing its intrinsic cosmological information" remains unsolved; ground-pickup and calibration systematics compound it.
  • G · Burba et al. — residual direction-dependent errors and ionospheric offsets "primarily constrain the upper limits" of CD/EoR power-spectrum measurements; deep-learning mitigation shows promise (correlation >0.92) but isn't yet a settled solution.
3of 7 sections

Ionospheric calibration sets a hard floor on low-frequency polarimetry, astrometry and EoR fidelity

BDG

Below roughly 350 MHz, the ionosphere becomes the limiting instrument, not the telescope. It caps rotation-measure precision and corrupts spectropolarimetry. Ionospheric calibration must be solved before EoR foreground removal can begin.

  • D · O'Sullivan et al. — residual ionospheric RM calibration error (~0.05 rad/m²) will dominate and prevent RM-precision gains even for high-S/N sources.
  • B · Patra et al. — direction-dependent instrumental effects and ionospheric phase distortions cause polarization-leakage that's difficult to calibrate out at low frequencies.
  • G · Datta, Morgan, Burba et al. — the ionosphere is "a primary limiting factor" on low-frequency interferometry; full characterisation of sub-km turbulent structure is not yet achieved.
3of 7 sections

Transient and compact-object progenitor physics is still unidentified

EFG

FRBs, long-period transients, and jetted tidal disruption events are detected in growing numbers, but their physical origins remain unsettled. Observational programmes are outrunning theory in this area.

  • F · Curtin et al. — the physical nature/progenitor of FRB sources remains open; it's unclear whether magnetars can explain all of them, implying multiple channels may be needed.
  • F · Shu et al. — the true abundance/rate of jetted TDEs is uncertain because they're easily misidentified as long GRBs or X-ray flashes.
  • G · Andersson et al. — the long-period-transient luminosity function is "currently unknown," and whether their steep turnover is intrinsic or extrinsic is undetermined.
3of 7 sections

PTA gravitational-wave-background origin — astrophysical or cosmological — remains undetermined

AEF

Three different working groups restate the same open question: does the nanohertz stochastic background come from supermassive black hole binaries, or from something cosmological? The "final parsec problem" for SMBH mergers remains unresolved under either explanation.

  • A · Bellomo & Cole — current PTAs have a small, anisotropically-distributed pulsar sample giving a "highly non-uniform sky response," with certain angular modes poorly constrained.
  • F · Shannon et al. — solar-system ephemeris uncertainty remains a limiting systematic; pulse jitter may set an ultimate timing floor independent of telescope sensitivity.
  • E · Cruz et al. — even joint SKAO+astrometry analyses "remain below the sensitivity required to detect the kinematic dipole."
3of 7 sections

VLBI network gaps constrain astrometric reach and imaging fidelity

CFG

Long-baseline science keeps running into the same three limits. There are too few southern-hemisphere stations and too few low-frequency baselines. Even once baselines exist, the field of view is too small to support wide surveys.

  • C · Rygl et al. — the predominantly northern-hemisphere distribution of existing VLBI facilities is flagged as a key limiting systematic only southern SKA-VLBI can resolve.
  • F · VLBI of compact objects sub-theme — small field of view is explicitly noted as a key VLBI limitation for large-area surveys; wide-field SKA-VLBI surveys remain an unsolved technical challenge.
  • G · Bempong-Manful; Timmerman/Kobayashi — converting legacy African antennas into working VLBI stations "has proven far more challenging than initially anticipated"; low-frequency long baselines are "very limited" at SKA-Low frequencies.
3of 7 sections

Rapid-response / triggering infrastructure isn't yet defined at the speed multi-messenger science needs

BFG

Researchers want SKAO to slew and start recording within seconds of an alert. No one has yet defined how fast that can actually happen, including the SKAO baseline design documents.

  • G · Anderson et al. — the SKA-Low repointing speed needed to catch coherent pre/during-merger signals (<20 s) is explicitly "not currently defined in the SKAO baseline documents."
  • G · Anderson et al. — sub-second triggering at 10 GHz for SKA-Mid is called "an unrealistic trigger time" given minutes-scale dish repointing.
  • B · burst-monitoring sub-theme — a persistent 15:00–20:00 UT gap in synoptic solar-burst coverage is flagged as needing to be filled.
2of 7 sections

Origin and amplification of cosmic magnetic fields remains unresolved at every scale

AE

Whether magnetic fields are primordial or dynamo-grown remains an open question, from the Milky Way to galaxy clusters. Cluster observations now show fields that are stronger, and appear earlier, than dynamo models predict.

  • A · Vernstrom et al. — the origin of cosmic magnetic fields is explicitly called "an open question"; the amplification mechanism "remains unresolved."
  • E · Loi et al. — observed µG-level cluster fields at z~0.8 "challenge models predicting slower dynamo growth and call for reassessment of early cluster magnetisation."
2of 7 sections

AGN jet launching, collimation and feedback coupling to gas is still mechanistically unresolved

DF

Decades of VLBI observation have not settled how jets are launched and collimated. It also remains unclear whether, or how, jets quench star formation as they propagate outward.

  • D · Maccagni et al. — explicitly flagged "key open questions": how outflows are driven from circumnuclear to circumgalactic scale, and how efficiently feedback impacts star formation.
  • D · Hardcastle et al. — no evidence yet of the energy dissipation expected if jets decelerate from core Γ~10 to kpc-scale Γ~1.5.
  • F · Spingola et al. — how SMBHs accrete mass fast enough to reach ~10¹⁰ M☉ within the first Gyr is "one of the biggest open questions" in SMBH growth; jet-assisted accretion is only a proposed, unconfirmed solution.
2of 7 sections

The Milky Way's own large-scale structure is still not pinned down

CD

After four centuries of study, the authors describe the Milky Way's own spiral pattern, rotation curve, and solar motion as "only roughly constrained." This is an inside-looking-out problem. More maser parallax measurements are needed to fix it.

  • C · Rygl et al. — competing 4-arm vs. 2-arm-plus-outer-arm spiral models remain unresolved; only one HMSFR maser parallax exists beyond ~20 kpc.
  • D · Xu et al. — solar height above the plane, solar peculiar motion, and the rotation curve at both R<4 kpc and R>12 kpc remain poorly constrained; the nature of the "3-kpc Arm" has been debated since 1957.

Machine-learning pipelines face a persistent sim-to-real domain gap

G

This issue is concentrated entirely in Methods & Techniques, but three separate papers raise it independently. The applications differ — deconvolution, source-finding, lensing/AI — but the underlying problem is the same: models trained on simulations do not reliably generalise to real SKA data.

  • G · Zhang & Li — deconvolution validated only in noise-free simulations; performance on real SKA-Low data not yet demonstrated.
  • G · Pal et al. — ML source-finders "depend heavily on the quality and diversity of their training datasets"; realistic labelled simulations remain an open problem.
  • G · Denzel et al. — sim-to-real domain gaps "remain a major challenge" across nearly all proposed AI applications in the chapter.

Notable section-specific unknowns (not cross-cutting, but worth flagging)

  • B — Definitive radio detection of star-planet magnetic interaction (M-SPI) remains elusive despite firm theoretical prediction (Vedantham).
  • B — The core initiation mechanism of lightning is unknown — measured thunderstorm fields never approach dielectric breakdown strength (Hare).
  • C — Whether pebble accretion can proceed under the turbulence driven by intense infall, crossing the "meter-size barrier," remains unclear (Wu/Garufi).
  • D — The origin of ORC-like circular diffuse radio sources remains contested among four competing scenarios, on too small a sample to decide (Pal).
  • E — Whether cosmological shear can be measured directly in the visibility plane, without full image reconstruction, is posed as an open question (Harrison).
  • F — Despite a decade of the most sensitive searches, no pulsar has been found orbiting Sgr A*; whether scattering or true absence explains it is debated (Abbate).
  • F — Hadronic-interaction properties governing air-shower development are "not precisely known," the dominant systematic on cosmic-ray mass measurements (Buitink).
  • G — Measuring an RM and its error for a single polarised source is called "an extremely complex problem that as yet has no optimal solution" (Broadband Polarimetry chapter).

The observing-mode contention map already showed that broad observing modes, such as wide continuum imaging or commensal transient monitoring, span 5 of 6 science sections. This pass asks a narrower question: which named techniques are load-bearing? Faraday rotation and polarimetry form the only technique that is genuinely both broad and deep. It is used materially in 6 of 7 sections, not just mentioned in passing. Everything else that looks broad in the contention map, including VLBI and transients, turns out at the technique level to be a family of related but distinct methods per section rather than one shared pipeline.
