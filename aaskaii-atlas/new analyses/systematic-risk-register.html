Systematic Risk Register

The Common Open Questions page (Part II·A) ranks unresolved physics — what causes the PTA background, what drives cosmic magnetism. This page is deliberately different: a register of named engineering and operational risks — calibration, RFI, compute cost, timing precision — that threaten data quality or feasibility regardless of what the science answer turns out to be. Scope & method

16 systematic/engineering keywords were run as independent searches across the corpus: RFI mitigation/excision, ionospheric calibration (engineering framing), direction-dependent calibration, dynamic-range limitation, confusion limit, polarization leakage, computational cost/scaling, data rate/throughput, survey-speed limitation, beam/PSF sidelobes, timing/clock synchronization, commensal scheduling conflict, telescope availability/uptime, and the ML sim-to-real domain gap. A risk only makes the register if papers state it as a real, named engineering constraint — not a passing mention — and ranking follows section-count first, paper-count second, the same rule used throughout this atlas. Two out-of-scope engineering documents that share this database (a design-baseline specification and an operations delivery plan) surfaced heavily on several of these searches; those are excluded from the counts below since they aren't AASKAII science-book papers, but are noted where a risk is thoroughly engineered-around there yet only thinly discussed in the 189-paper corpus itself.

RFI mitigation / excision

BEG

Named across solar radio science, intensity-mapping, and methods papers as a data-corrupting nuisance requiring active real-time countermeasures, independent of the science target. 6 qualifying papers.

  • G · ML and the SKA for Cosmic Dawn/EoR — "RFI represents a major obstacle to the detection and characterization of the cosmological 21cm signal... its highly variable nature... can mimic or obscure genuine astronomical signals."
  • B · Solar/Heliospheric Calibration & Imaging Framework — dedicates §6.6 to distinguishing RFI from active solar emission, proposing thinned visibility recording since full-rate RFI-clean recording isn't tractable.
  • E · Fast Simultaneous Surveys with On-the-Fly Mapping — describes excising "rogue antennas" that fall behind during fast scanning as a concrete operational RFI-adjacent step.
3of 7 sections

VLBI timing, clock synchronisation & correlator precision

BFG

Achieving the microarcsecond precision VLBI science needs depends on synchronisation, digitisation, and correlator sensitivity infrastructure — an engineering floor distinct from any single science case. 5 qualifying papers.

  • G · VLBI with SKAMPI — the Effelsberg Direct Digitisation backend covers "the full signal chain... time distribution, digitisation, packetisation, and backend signal processing," built specifically to derisk this chain.
  • G · SKA VLBI Survey for Astrometry, Geodesy & Astrophysics — requires calibration against co-located GNSS receivers to hold geodetic precision.
  • B · Ultra-Precise Astrometric Search for Exoplanets with SKA-VLBI — the detection floor is set by VLBA-class single-epoch precision (~100 µas), an instrumental ceiling, not a physics unknown.

Polarisation leakage / instrumental-polarisation calibration accuracy

BCG

The precision with which instrumental polarisation response must be characterised, independent of what Zeeman or coronal signal is being chased. 4 qualifying papers.

  • C · Search for Seeds of The Cradle of Stars — a whole subsection, "The Required Accuracy of Polarization Calibration," covers the precision needed to detect a Zeeman/Stokes-V signal at the ~0.05 K level.
  • B · Coronal Magnetography via Spectropolarimetry — ties coronal-field measurement fidelity to attenuator/receiver polarimetric calibration precision (<1 dB / ±2%).
  • G · Foreground Characterisation and Mitigation in CD/EoR — treats leakage of Galactic synchrotron into Stokes I as a foreground-removal systematic requiring "extremely well-characterized instrumental responses."
2of 7 sections

Computational cost / compute scaling / SDP processing burden

EG

A named tension between the raw scale of SKA data and the resolution/inference power science actually needs from it. 5 qualifying papers.

  • G · Source Finding and Characterisation for SKAO Science — "the SDP will be among the largest HPC infrastructures ever built, processing exabytes of raw visibilities into science-ready catalogues."
  • G · The Role of Artificial Intelligence in the SKA Era — "a persistent computational tension... between the need for cosmological volumes... and the sub-kpc resolution required," motivating AI-based deconvolution as cost mitigation.
  • G · A Non-Negativity Iterative Approach to Image Deconvolution — built explicitly to reduce this cost (512×512 image in 1–2s on a laptop, quoted as a design target).

Beam / PSF sidelobe artefacts

EG

Imaging-fidelity systematics that threaten flagship products (weak lensing, source catalogues) regardless of the cosmology or population being measured. 5 qualifying papers.

  • G · Weak Lensing with the SKAO: Radio Shear Measurement — "the multiplicative bias exceeds it by roughly an order of magnitude... likely reflects limitations of the shear estimator itself."
  • E · Methodological Frontiers in 21-cm Intensity Mapping — §6.1 treats beam sidelobe structure as a named systematic requiring dedicated mitigation alongside foreground cleaning.
  • G · Commensal Image Plane Transient Search Methods — sparse uv-coverage in short integrations "produces irregular point spread functions and structured noise, generating many artefacts."

Direction-dependent calibration — overfitting/signal-suppression risk

EG

Deliberately narrower than the ionosphere entry below: this is about DD calibration's own free-parameter count risking eating the astrophysical signal, whatever causes the DD errors in the first place. 3 qualifying papers.

  • E · CD/EoR Observational Lessons Learned — "DD calibration involves a large number of free parameters, which increases the risk of overfitting and suppressing the 21-cm signal."
  • G · Foreground Characterisation and Mitigation in CD/EoR — §3.3.2 treats DDEs as a distinct pipeline stage requiring joint source-subtraction/calibration solving.

Ionosphere as a calibration/dynamic-range engineering constraint

G

Quantitative dynamic-range-vs-frequency behaviour, distinct from the science-limiting framing on the existing open-questions page.

  • G · Studying Ionosphere Using SKA-Low and SKA-Mid — derives quantitative dynamic-range degradation formulas from ionospheric phase error (δTEC) vs. frequency and averaging.
  • G · Foreground Characterisation and Mitigation in CD/EoR — "the dynamic range required is formidable," driven directly by ionospheric turbulence.

How this differs from the open-questions page: that page covers ionospheric calibration as a limit on what science conclusions are reachable. This entry reports the mechanical, quantitative engineering behaviour of the same effect — a calibration/data-quality problem that exists for SKA-Low regardless of which science case is running. Same physical cause, different register: "how do you calibrate around this" vs. "what does this stop us learning."

Sim-to-real / ML generalisation gap (production-readiness framing)

G

Confirmed independently as an operational-trust/robustness problem for deploying ML in production pipelines, not a scientific-mystery framing.

  • G · The Role of Artificial Intelligence in the SKA Era — U-Net deconvolution "trading extreme inference speed against risks of hallucinations and poor generalization."
  • G · ML and the SKA for Cosmic Dawn/EoR — "rigorous validation against simulated datasets will be crucial... to transition from research prototypes... to operational components."

Data rate / throughput / archive volume

G

The 189-paper corpus treats this only in passing — the much more detailed data-volume/cost analysis (multi-exabyte storage, ~19,000 PB/yr uncompressed) lives in the out-of-scope engineering design-baseline documents, not the science book itself.

  • G · Source Finding and Characterisation for SKAO Science — "these instruments collectively produce petabyte-scale data products... processing exabytes of raw visibilities."

Searched, unconfirmed or thin

Reported explicitly rather than silently omitted

  • Telescope uptime / availability / maintenance risk — extensive treatment exists (reliability/availability/maintainability analysis), but entirely in the out-of-scope engineering design-baseline and delivery-plan documents. Zero confirmed statements from the 189 AASKAII science-book papers themselves.
  • Survey-speed limitation / field-of-view tradeoff — every hit from in-scope papers scored low relevance; no paper explicitly frames survey speed as a named constraint rather than a capability to exploit. Unconfirmed.
  • Confusion limit — one clearly in-scope hit only: Cosmology from Clustering of Continuum Galaxies (E) notes a wide-area survey design "overcoming the effects of confusion which affects deep surveys such as MIGHTEE." Real, but a single paper/section — too thin to rank in the top register.
  • Commensal scheduling conflict — two G-section papers (SETI; Commensal Image Plane Transient Search Methods) discuss commensality's technical tradeoffs, but both cluster in one section — noted, not promoted given the section-count ranking rule.

Sourcing discipline: a risk only appears above where papers state it as a real, confirmed constraint; where a keyword search came back thin or unconfirmed, that is reported directly (see above) rather than silently dropped.

Scope boundary: an SKA1 engineering design-baseline specification and an SKA Observatory delivery plan share this database but are not among the 189 AASKAII science-book papers — hits from those two were excluded from every count above, but are noted inline wherever a risk is thoroughly engineered-around there while only thinly discussed in the science book itself (data rate/throughput; telescope uptime).
