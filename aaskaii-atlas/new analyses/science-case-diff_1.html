Scope & method

The astronomy-rag corpus turns out to hold the 2015 book too, distinguishable from AASKAII by a collection tag on every retrieved passage — (Advancing Astrophysics with the Square Kilometre Array, 2015) versus (AASKAII, 2026) — even though the document-listing tool doesn't surface that split on its own. Each of the 2015 book's section headers was searched directly for its dominant science drivers and for explicit open/unresolved-problem statements, then diffed against the 2026 cross-cutting-methods ranking and open-questions tiers already established elsewhere in this atlas.

Open a decade, still open

Six problems the 2026 open-questions page ranks as tier-2 or tier-3 turn out to have a direct, explicit 2015 statement of the same unresolved issue — not a vague ancestor, a near-identical claim.

VLBI network gaps — too few southern/African baselines

2015 · Synergies2026 · C/F/G, tier-3

Paragi et al. (2015): "Key to the success of this approach will be the addition of a few (2-4) remote stations in Africa... to give good uv-coverage."

2026 produced a direct-descendant chapter for exactly this gap: Bempong-Manful et al., "Extending the SKA Across Africa" — a decade-old infrastructure ask that spawned its own follow-on paper rather than closing.

Rapid-response / triggering infrastructure undefined

2015 · The Transient Universe2026 · B/F/G, tier-3

Fender et al. (2015): "Before 2012, no robotic override system... had ever been operated on a radio telescope... we consider it essential that the SKA implement such modes."

2026's Anderson et al. still names SKA-Low repointing speed and SKA-Mid triggering latency as "not currently defined in the SKAO baseline documents" — the same design gap, still open.

FRB / compact-object progenitor physics unidentified

2015 · The Transient Universe2026 · E/F/G, tier-3

Macquart et al. (2015): FRB scattering origin "as yet, undetermined" between host-galaxy ISM and intergalactic medium.

2026 (Curtin et al., Qiu et al. on LPTs) still can't resolve the same IGM-vs-host ambiguity for the class's key observables.

Origin and amplification of cosmic magnetic fields

2015 · Magnetism2026 · A/E, tier-2

Johnston-Hollitt et al. (2015): SKA2-era goal explicitly framed as "the origin and amplification of cosmic magnetic fields"; Bonafede et al. (2015) makes the origin question its paper title outright.

Essentially unchanged in framing across a decade — 2026's tier-2 item restates the same open question almost verbatim.

EoR astrophysical-source uncertainty (feeds foreground contamination)

2015 · Epoch of Reionisation2026 · A/E/G, tier-3

Koopmans et al. (2015): "very little is known about [the history of neutral hydrogen]... only a handful of observations." Iliev & Semelin (2015): early-source physics "still highly uncertain."

Maps directly onto 2026's still-open foreground-removal problem for 21-cm/HI cosmology.

Dark-matter substructure / missing-satellites tension

2015 · The Hydrogen Universe2026 · E

2015 (Hydrogen Universe summary): ΛCDM substructure predictions "an order of magnitude larger than the numbers of satellite galaxies observed."

Same tension persists directly into 2026's Lelli et al. dark-matter/modified-gravity chapter.

One near-miss: PTA gravitational-wave origin

2026's tier-3 framing is specifically "astrophysical-binary vs. cosmological origin of the GW background, undetermined." The 2015 chapter (Janssen et al.) discusses PTA/GW only as a sensitivity/precision problem — pulsar-distance accuracy limiting angular resolution — not as an origin-ambiguity question. The science driver is continuous across both books, but this particular open-question framing appears to have sharpened considerably since 2015 rather than being carried over unchanged. Reported as "not stated" for 2015 rather than assumed.

How nine legacy chapters became seven AASKAII sections

The single strongest pattern in this diff: a standalone 2015 working-group chapter can become either a distributed cross-cutting technique, an absorbed sub-theme, or — twice — a topic with no 2026 chapter-level home at all.

2015 chapter2026 fate
Magnetism19 papers→ACDEG — became 2026's single most cross-cutting technique (Faraday rotation/polarimetry, rank 1 of 16), not a section at all.
The Cradle of Life12 papers→C disks/YSOs · B exoplanet radio · G SETI — dissolved three ways, no 2026 astrobiology chapter survives; SETI demoted from dedicated science chapter to a "Methods" entry.
The Continuum Universe19 papers→E SZ/lensing · D AGN/continuum — strong gravitational lensing has no 2026 chapter; a 2026 overview cites the 2015 chapter directly (McKean et al.) rather than restating the case.
Cosmology + Epoch of Reionisation18 + 14 papers→E — two separate 2015 WGs merged into one 2026 section, "The Cosmos."
Fundamental Physics w/ Pulsars11 papers→F — absorbed as one flavour of "extreme" astrophysics, losing standalone WG identity.
The Hydrogen Universe10 papers→D · A — folded in; angular-momentum science (Obreschkow 2015) has no clear 2026 heir, a candidate genuinely-dropped driver.
Synergies and Other Science19 papers→G VLBI · F multi-messenger — no 2026 counterpart section survives as a named whole.
The Transient Universe12 papers→F · A overview — direct content continuity, loss of standalone-WG status.

A dropped design philosophy, not just a dropped science case

Wilkinson's 2015 chapter "The SKA and the Unknown Unknowns" argued explicitly that SKA's design should reserve capability margin for serendipitous discovery — a first-class design-philosophy chapter tracing back to the 2004 SKA science case. No 2026 equivalent was found; the closest is a passing "opening new discovery space" mention inside one transients overview, far narrower and not framed as a standalone argument. Of everything found in this diff, this is the clearest case of a chapter-level idea being dropped rather than merely reorganised.

Coverage: each of the 10 legacy sections received 2–6 targeted searches — enough to establish dominant drivers and at least one explicit open-question quote per section, but not a paper-by-paper audit of all 135 legacy papers. A first-pass null result on some queries (Magnetism, Continuum-Universe, generic VLBI) was corrected by re-querying with title-anchored phrasing — the corpus's hybrid ranker sometimes favoured 2026 passages even on searches aimed squarely at 2015 topics, so an initial empty result was never treated as proof of absence.

Paper-count note: this pass found 135 legacy papers (134 excluding the Braun et al. overview) against this atlas's working estimate of "~157" — reported as the exact tool output rather than reconciled to the earlier estimate, per this project's sourcing rule of reporting what a search actually returns.

One flagged non-finding: the "angular momentum" driver (Obreschkow 2015) may simply be discussed inside a 2026 paper's body text without a title match — flagged as a candidate dropped driver, not a certainty.
