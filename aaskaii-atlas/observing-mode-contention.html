Scope & method

This analysis covers the AASKAII book (Advancing Astrophysics with the SKAO II), the current 2026-generation SKA Observatory science case. It spans seven thematic sections and roughly 189 papers. The 2015 AASKA I legacy chapters — Cosmology, Epoch of Reionisation, Magnetism, Fundamental Physics with Pulsars, and others — are excluded from this pass.

The analysis searched each section below against the SKAO document corpus, using hybrid semantic and keyword retrieval over the paper text, and condensed the results into representative sub-themes. Every sub-theme is tagged against a fixed 15-category observing-mode taxonomy. The same categories apply consistently across every section. As a result, overlaps in the matrix reflect genuinely shared vocabulary rather than coincidentally similar wording.

  • Science drivers — the 2–4 questions or goals the sub-theme is built around.
  • Sensitivity & resolution — concrete rms, angular/spectral resolution, or timing-precision figures quoted in the source papers.
  • Frequency / redshift range — the band(s) and target-distance range invoked.
  • Observing mode(s) — which of the 15 standard SKA observing modes the science depends on, colour-coded by which array it draws on.

HOTTEST — 5 of 6 sections×3-way tie

Wide/ultra-deep continuum & RM polarimetry

Five sections claim the same deep, wide continuum imaging product: AGN and galaxy evolution (D), star formation and the Galactic plane (C), solar wide-field monitoring (B), the continuum overview WG (A), and cosmology clustering (E). RM-grid polarimetry shows the identical five-section spread, from solar CME B-fields to cluster magnetism. These two modes are the most oversubscribed shared products in the book.

ALSO HOTTEST5 of 6

Transient / commensal / ToO monitoring

Solar bursts (B), magnetar outbursts (C), the radio transients overview (A), FRB cosmology (E), and the entire Extreme Universe section (F) all need commensal, always-on transient capture. The science differs across these five cases, but the infrastructure demand is identical: buffering, triggering, and rapid response.

CROSS-ARRAY4 of 6

VLBI / astrometry

Four otherwise unrelated sections seek VLBI/astrometry: exoplanet and TNO astrometry (B), maser and magnetar parallaxes (C), AGN and Galactic magnetism (D), and compact-object and jet imaging (F). All four compete for the same finite long-baseline scheduling and correlator time.

NARROW BUT DEEP2 of 6

EoR / Cosmic Dawn 21-cm

Only the WG overview (A) and Cosmos (E) sections directly drive this mode. Despite that narrow base, EoR/Cosmic Dawn 21-cm draws the heaviest Methods & Techniques investment in the book: ionosphere calibration, foreground removal, and machine learning are all built around it. This mode has a narrow science base but an outsized infrastructure footprint.

UNCONTESTED1 of 6, or 0

HI intensity mapping, weak lensing, solar imaging, SETI

Deep HI intensity mapping belongs to Cosmos alone. Weak lensing also belongs to Cosmos alone. Solar/heliospheric dynamic imaging belongs to Sun & Planets alone. SETI has no dedicated science-section time at all; it rides entirely commensally via Methods & Techniques.

Corpus: This analysis covers AASKAII (Advancing Astrophysics with the SKAO II): 7 thematic sections and approximately 189 papers. It searched the papers using hybrid semantic and BM25 retrieval with cross-encoder re-ranking. The 2015 AASKA I legacy chapters were excluded from this pass by design.

Method: Sections D, E, and F (49, 43, and 34 papers respectively) were too large to tabulate paper-by-paper, so each was compressed into 5–7 representative sub-themes. Sections A, B, C, and G were small enough to tabulate at near-full granularity. Sensitivity and resolution figures are quoted directly from the source papers where the papers state them. A blank cell means the paper did not state a figure explicitly; it does not mean no such figure exists.

Taxonomy: The 15 observing-mode categories were fixed before research began and applied identically in every pass. As a result, the matrix reflects a shared, consistent vocabulary rather than post-hoc pattern-matching across differently worded chapters.
