A 100-Hour HI Observing Proposal

A single prompt, "Write an observing proposal for a small 100-hour program looking at HI in galaxies using AA*", produced the draft below from the AASKAII corpus and the live sensitivity calculator. Version 1 published it without rerunning its figures; this version reruns them through the calculator and the setup validator, and the sensitivity budget survives the check.

The science case

Neutral hydrogen extends farther from a galaxy than any other gas component and is disturbed more easily, which makes it the earliest tracer of tidal and ram-pressure stripping as a galaxy falls into a cluster. The draft therefore requests resolved 21-cm imaging of twelve galaxies in the outskirts and infall region of the Fornax cluster at about 20 Mpc, aiming to map tidal tails, warps and gas tails at a resolution and depth beyond what MeerKAT, ASKAP and WSRT have reached. The sample is illustrative rather than pre-selected, and the investigator fields are placeholders; the page presents the draft as a demonstration of what the corpus supports, not as a vetted request.

What the check shows

The draft's central number holds. With the AA* inner 2-km subarray, a 4 MHz zoom window of 13.44 kHz channels and six hours on source, the calculator returns 250 µJy/beam per channel at robust 0 with a 15.4″ × 12.5″ beam, within two per cent of the draft's 254 µJy/beam and its quoted beam; no other weighting comes within ten per cent, which confirms that the draft assumed robust 0 even though it names the weighting only in passing. The smoothed noise and the column-density limit follow from that figure by standard scaling, and both reproduce.

The setup is valid in the SV-AA*, Cycle 0 and Cycle 1 contexts but not in SV-AA2, where zoom mode is unavailable and the inner 2-km template does not yet exist; consequently the programme could run only once AA* science verification begins. Two items remain unchecked. The HI-mass limit depends on an assumed line width that the draft does not state, and the draft defines no output data products, so no data-volume estimate can be made without inventing them.

One practical lesson for users of the calculator tools: the calculator's zoom endpoint accepts the subarray label ("AA* (MID_inner_r2km)"), not the name ("MID_inner_r2km_aastar") that its subarray list also returns; the name is refused with a validation error.
