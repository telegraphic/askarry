Time Demand & LST Pressure

This page adds up the observing time that AASKAII chapters request. Each request was reviewed against its quoted source, and restatements of the same survey were merged. Where a chapter states where on the sky a request points, the page spreads its hours across local sidereal time (LST) for SKA-Low and SKA-Mid.

Which surveys fill the sidereal day

The pressure in each one-hour LST bin is the requested time divided by the time available in that bin over the planning horizon. Each colour shows one survey tier or request, and the red line marks a pressure of 1, where demand would equal supply.

Where the hours go

Each segment shows one accepted request as a share of its telescope's total, with the largest first. Coloured segments have a stated position on the sky. Grey segments are counted but not placed, because no chapter states where they point.

Reference survey tiers

Each row is one distinct survey tier, with the chapter that defines it and the chapters that restate it. The hours in brackets are those that a restating chapter gives. The review kept them for the record but did not add them to the total.

What the numbers mean

Each request was extracted from a chapter together with a word-for-word quote, and then reviewed. The review kept one row for every distinct survey tier and marked each restatement of that tier as a duplicate of the chapter that defines it. It rejected modes that need no dedicated time and sensitivity demonstrations that propose no observation. It also corrected the hours where a chapter gave a figure per year or per event. Requests whose footprint a chapter states were placed on the sky. The rest are counted but not placed, because spreading a survey of unknown position evenly over the sky would invent a pressure pattern that the book does not support.

The pressure in each LST bin is the placed demand divided by the time available in that bin over the planning horizon, after a share is set aside for maintenance. It measures which science cases compete for the same sidereal time. It is not an allocation, nor a forecast of oversubscription. Moreover, the operational settings behind it are unverified defaults that still need an owner at the SKAO. Requests in the same commensal group share their observing time. Each group is therefore drawn once, at the larger of its members' demands in each cell.
