Time Demand & LST Pressure

Version 1 of the atlas could say only how often each observing mode is mentioned. This page answers the scheduling question directly: it collects the observing time that AASKAII chapters request, reviews each request against its quoted source, merges restatements of the same reference survey, and computes how the placed demand falls across local sidereal time for SKA-Low and SKA-Mid.

What the numbers mean

Each request was extracted from a chapter with a verbatim quote and then reviewed. The review kept one row for every distinct survey tier, marked each restatement of that tier as a duplicate of its defining paper, rejected modes that need no dedicated time and sensitivity demonstrations that propose no observation, and corrected hours where a paper stated a per-year or per-event figure. Requests whose footprint a paper states were placed on the sky; the rest are counted but left unplaced, because spreading a survey of unknown position evenly over the sky would manufacture a pressure pattern the book does not support.

The pressure in each LST bin is the placed demand divided by the time available in that bin over the planning horizon, after a maintenance share is removed. It is therefore a relative measure of which science cases compete for the same sidereal time, not an allocation and not a forecast of oversubscription, and the operational settings behind it are unverified defaults awaiting an owner at the SKAO.
