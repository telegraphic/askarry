Target & Field Atlas

The chapters name several hundred objects and dozens of survey fields. Each object name was resolved against SIMBAD, and each survey footprint was recorded only where a chapter states it. The map therefore shows where on the sky the book's science is actually aimed.

Reading the map

The book's attention concentrates where the southern sites give the best access. The Galactic Centre is the most often named object, cited by thirty-one chapters, followed by the COSMOS field and the two Magellanic Clouds. The Galactic plane appears as a dense stripe of named sources across the southern sky. Nevertheless, the map shows how much of the book's attention lies beyond easy reach. Of the 301 objects with positions, 125 sit north of Dec +14°, and no source that far north reaches 45° elevation at the SKA-Mid site. Cygnus A and the Andromeda Galaxy, which a dozen or more chapters each name, are among them. Many of these are comparison objects from northern-hemisphere work rather than SKA targets. However, where a chapter does request time on one, the time-demand page lists the request as never visible, unless a lower elevation limit is accepted.

The names were resolved automatically, so the results are imperfect. SIMBAD sometimes returns a catalogue designation instead of the familiar name; Cygnus A, for example, appears under its MCG number. A few names could not be resolved at all.
