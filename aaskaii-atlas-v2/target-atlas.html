Target & Field Atlas

The chapters name several hundred individual objects and dozens of survey fields. Each object name was extracted from the text and resolved against SIMBAD, and each survey footprint was recorded only when a chapter states it, so the map below shows where on the sky the book's science is actually aimed.

Reading the map

The book's attention concentrates where the southern sites give it the best access. The Galactic Centre is the most frequently named object, cited by thirty-one chapters, and the COSMOS field and the two Magellanic Clouds follow; the Galactic plane appears as a dense stripe of named sources through the southern sky. The map nevertheless shows how much of the book's attention lies beyond easy reach: 125 of the 301 objects with positions sit north of Dec +14°, above which no source reaches 45° elevation at the SKA-Mid site, and Cygnus A and the Andromeda Galaxy, each named by a dozen or more chapters, are among them. Many of these are comparison objects from northern-hemisphere work rather than SKA targets, but where a chapter does request time on one, the request appears as never visible on the time-demand page unless a lower elevation limit is accepted.

Resolution is automatic and therefore imperfect. SIMBAD sometimes returns a catalogue designation rather than the familiar name (Cygnus A, for example, appears under its MCG number), and a small number of names could not be resolved at all.
