Citation Landscape

The reference lists of the AASKAII chapters show which earlier works the book builds on. For every cited work, the page counts how many chapters cite it. This count separates the shared foundations of the science case from the literature of individual communities.

What the book stands on

The most widely cited work is the paper by Braun and collaborators on the anticipated performance of SKA Phase 1, which about forty chapters cite. It is the common source of sensitivities across the book, which explains why so many chapters quote figures that agree with one another. The next tier holds the reference papers of the precursor and pathfinder surveys, above all LOFAR and its Two-metre Sky Survey. It also holds the survey definitions of the Magnetism Science Working Group and the reviews of radio emission from galaxy clusters. Several AASKAII chapters cite one another as well, and these appear in the list as works dated 2026.

The pattern changes from section to section. The solar and planetary chapters cite the LOFAR instrument paper and Kontar's studies of how the corona scatters radio waves. The stellar-formation chapters cite the review of high-mass star formation by Motte and collaborators. The extreme-universe chapters cite the ATNF pulsar catalogue of Manchester and collaborators. A script parses the reference strings automatically, so a few entries take their author from a journal abbreviation. The table therefore shows each reference as written, so that you can judge each entry on its own.
