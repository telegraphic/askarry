What the counts show

The SKA precursors and pathfinders dominate the network, as one would expect of a book written by the communities that operate them. A large share of the chapters in every section name MeerKAT and LOFAR, and these two share more chapters than any other pair. Beyond radio astronomy, the pattern is more informative. JWST, Planck and Euclid each appear in about forty chapters. However, Planck and Euclid are concentrated in the cosmology chapters, whereas Rubin/LSST is the only facility outside radio astronomy that all seven sections name. Gaia shows the same pattern on a smaller scale. Only fifteen chapters name it, little more than a third as many as name JWST, yet it reaches six sections, as JWST does. Its science therefore touches most parts of the book rather than serving one community in depth.

Two groups of facilities that a reader might expect to be missing are in fact present. Ten chapters across three sections name the ngVLA, mostly in work on galaxy evolution and the extreme universe. The gamma-ray facilities appear as well. CTAO appears in twelve chapters across five sections, and Fermi in six chapters across three, with the extreme-universe section accounting for most of both. Both groups therefore belong in the synergy picture. In contrast, no chapter names a handful of the seventy-eight curated facilities more than once. The foot of the page lists them, so that their absence is recorded rather than implied.
