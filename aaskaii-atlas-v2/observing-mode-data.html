Modes & Data Products

The SKAO configures every observation in one of four broad modes: continuum imaging, zoom spectroscopy, pulsar search (PSS) and pulsar timing (PST). This page sorts the book's reviewed time requests into those four modes. It then lists the output data products that each mode produces and estimates how much data each request would generate.

The four modes in the book

Each cell gives the number of chapters in a section that name at least one of a mode's search terms twice. The table below the matrix shows which of the fifteen fine-grained modes on the Observing-Mode Map belong to each broad mode.

How the estimates were made

Each request was assigned to a mode by keyword rules applied to its name, its quote and its review note. Pulsar timing comes first, then pulsar search, then VLBI, then spectral-line work, and every remaining request counts as continuum. A spectral-line request also counts as continuum when its text says so. After the rules had run, every assignment was read once, and six were corrected by hand; the corrections and their reasons are kept in REQUEST_CATEGORY_OVERRIDES in scripts/build_atlas_data.py.

The volumes come from the data-product models in rag/vendor/setup_validator/data_products.py, the newest copy of the SKAO model code. Each request uses the full AA* array of its telescope. Its frequency is the one the request states, or the centre of its band, or else a default. The image resolution is the wavelength divided by the longest baseline, and the field is the primary beam, 1.1 λ/D. Where a chapter states a survey area, the image instead covers that whole area as one mosaic, so its pixel count matches the sky it maps.

The visibilities are averaged as far as smearing allows. Time and frequency averaging stop when either would smear a source at the half-power radius by a quarter of the synthesised beam, and both factors stay within the schema's limit of 1 to 1000. Continuum visibilities span the band's default bandwidth. A zoom window spans 4000 channels at the widest zoom channel width, which is 13.44 kHz for SKA-Mid and 1.808 kHz for SKA-Low. Pulsar search output follows the model's fixed rate for folded candidates. Pulsar timing produces one archive per hour on source, with 10-second sub-integrations.

Several requests often describe one commensal observation, as with the tiers of a reference survey. In that case each product is counted once for the group, at the size of its largest member. This matches how the time-demand page treats commensal groups.
