Capability Cross-Check

A requirement matters only if some stage of the telescope can meet it. This page asks, for every requirement in the register, whether the band, resolution, bandwidth or mode it needs is available in the observing contexts the SKAO has defined for science verification and the first cycles, needs the full AA4 design, or lies outside what is planned at all.

How the verdicts were reached

Static capability documents describe the full design, whereas the setup validator enforces the limits of each defined context, so the checking agent consulted the validator's schemas and rules for the SV-AA2, SV-AA*, Cycle 0 and Cycle 1 contexts first and used the capability documents only for what the schemas do not cover. A requirement is marked available at AA* when the schemas allow its configuration in an AA* or early-cycle context, AA4-only when it needs the longest baselines, the full dish count or another AA4 feature, outside the current contexts when neither the contexts nor the design baseline provide it, and not addressed when both sources are silent. Silence is never read as support, which corrects the version 1 page, whose verdicts drew on documents alone.

Verdicts

What the verdicts show

Just over half of the register, 239 of 456 requirements, can be met in an AA* or early-cycle context, and a further 186 need the full AA4 design, so the book's stated demands divide almost evenly between what the first observing cycles can deliver and what must wait for the complete array. The balance shifts from section to section. The stellar-formation chapters and the methods chapters lean toward AA4, because resolving disk rings, jet-launching regions and long VLBI baselines requires the longest baselines, whereas the solar and cosmology chapters lean toward AA*, whose surface-brightness sensitivity and field of view already serve most of their cases.

Seventeen requirements fall outside every defined context and the design baseline, and they cluster around the gaps in frequency coverage rather than around sensitivity. Water masers at 22 GHz and the proposed Band 6 above 15.4 GHz account for several; the 1.76–4.6 GHz gap left by the unbuilt Bands 3 and 4 accounts for several more; the rest ask for SKA-Low above 350 MHz, 14-Hz zoom channels on SKA-Low, or a 400 MHz transient buffer. Consequently, the register identifies receiver coverage, not collecting area, as the main place where the science case outruns the planned telescope.
